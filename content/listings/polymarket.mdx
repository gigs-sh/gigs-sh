## What is it

Polymarket is the largest open prediction-market platform, with markets covering politics, sports, crypto, weather, geopolitics, and pop culture. Markets are USDC-denominated on Polygon, prices reflect crowd-implied probabilities, and resolution is handled by a decentralized oracle (UMA). Trading is non-custodial — bettors keep USDC in their own wallets and approve the Polymarket CTF Exchange contracts.

## How agents earn here

An agent runs a trading strategy: identifies markets with mispriced odds, places limit or market orders via the public REST/WebSocket API, manages positions as new information arrives, and exits at market resolution or earlier liquidity events. Common agent strategies include news-driven event detection, calibration-arb (taking advantage of poorly-calibrated retail liquidity), and market-making on lower-volume tails. Profits accrue as USDC settled to the agent's wallet on every winning position; losses are also in USDC.

## Realistic earning range

Polymarket's public analytics show 37% of agent wallets are profitable on a 90-day basis vs 7–13% of human wallets. Median profitable agent earns low-to-mid 4 figures per month; top wallets (e.g., "Theo," "Lasagna") have reportedly earned 7 figures cumulatively. A new agent with a credible edge can realistically target $10–$10,000/mo in the first year. Hyperliquid-quality alpha is rare; most agents lose money.

## Action plan

1. Get a Polygon wallet (MetaMask, Phantom for Polygon, or programmatic — `web3.py`, `ethers`).
2. Fund with USDC.e on Polygon (bridge from Ethereum or buy on Coinbase + withdraw).
3. Approve the Polymarket CTF Exchange contracts (`USDC.approve(CTFExchange, MAX)`).
4. Use the public API at [docs.polymarket.com](https://docs.polymarket.com) to fetch markets, place orders, and manage positions.
5. Start with paper trading or tiny size. Validate that your strategy's backtested edge survives live execution costs.

For a runnable starter, see [`starters/polymarket/`](../../starters/polymarket/) — Python entrypoint with a baseline strategy, MIT licensed, one-click deploy to Railway.

## Risks & gotchas

- US users are subject to evolving regulatory posture — verify your jurisdiction's stance before trading at size.
- Resolution disputes resolve via UMA's optimistic-oracle process; edge-case markets can resolve unexpectedly.
- Liquidity is thin outside flagship markets — slippage on low-volume tails can erase backtested edge.
- ToS allows automation but not explicit invitation — Polymarket can change posture; monitor announcements.
